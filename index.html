About

I am a Ph.D. student in Economics at the University of Miami (2024–2029, expected), with research interests in macro-finance. I hold an M.S. in Applied Economics from Johns Hopkins University and a B.A. in Economics from Renmin University of China.

My current work focuses on credit card debt, global reserve currency dynamics, and the macro-financial consequences of financial-sector expansion. Ongoing projects include a dynamic discrete-choice model of credit-card debt resolution, a study of de-dollarization in global central bank reserves, and an investigation of how financial expansion affects export product quality in Chinese manufacturing firms.

Prior to my doctoral studies, I worked as a research assistant at the Chinese University of Hong Kong (Shenzhen).

De-Dollarization of Central Bank Reserves in the World Economy: 2015–2025
Co-author — University of Miami.
Journal of Risk and Financial Management (Forthcoming).
Examines de-dollarization in central bank reserve portfolios over 2015–2025 by explicitly incorporating gold into total reserves and applying a reserve-accounting framework inspired by Solow-style decomposition. Using data for the ten largest official gold holders outside the United States, the paper separates active portfolio reallocation from passive valuation effects and shows that the decline in the U.S. dollar’s reserve share becomes substantially larger once gold is included. The results indicate that, for most countries, observed de-dollarization is driven more by gold-price appreciation and valuation effects than by large active reductions in dollar holdings, although active gold accumulation is important in cases such as Russia, China, India, and Japan.
